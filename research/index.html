01 / Recursive self-improvement

Measure improvement in the process itself

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) research asks whether a system can change the methods it uses to solve problems and improve through those changes. A higher score alone does not establish this: extra compute, an easier task distribution, or a mismatched control can also produce an apparent gain.

For the definition, a sourced history from I. J. Good to 2026 systems, and a checklist of confounds, read what recursive self-improvement is and how to test a claim.

This project studies bounded, testable forms of self-improvement. It does not claim to have demonstrated artificial general intelligence or open-ended recursive improvement.

Projects and evidence

  • gated-self-improvement: an LLM-free, deterministic engine with counterfactual controls and matched compute. Read its claim boundaries before interpreting a result.
  • rsi-bench: a multi-axis benchmark covering self-modification depth, improvement trajectories, operator discovery, meta-adaptation, safety, and autonomy.

Why matched controls matter: an earlier compounding-improvement claim in gated-self-improvement relied on a 5× compute mismatch. The original claim was retracted. The published postmortem remains part of the research record.

02 / Neural architecture search

Search for attention-free sequence models

Neural architecture search explores candidate model structures rather than assuming that the first hand-designed architecture is the best one. The attention-free-sequence-model project searches combinations of atomic mechanisms for sequence modeling.

The reported search covers 3,000 candidate architectures across 45 atomic mechanisms. Its search winner scored 97.6%, compared with 64.8% for the transformer baseline, on three synthetic long-range tasks.

Scope of the result: sequence lengths were 32–64. These numbers do not establish superiority over transformers on real-world language modeling. Matched comparisons against Mamba, RWKV, and Hyena have not yet been reported here. See the limitations and evidence.

Next evaluation

The planned next step is evaluation on a real text corpus at sequence lengths of 512–1024, with subquadratic baselines trained under a matched budget. This is a research plan, not a completed result.

03 / Program synthesis

Put verification inside the search loop

Program synthesis generates candidate programs from a search space. In validation-gated synthesis, a candidate must pass explicit checks before it is accepted. The aim is to distinguish a useful change from an artifact of the tasks, search procedure, or evaluation.

rsi-metaforge-core explores meta-meta learning loops, analogical transfer, grammar-mediated expansion, and anti-cheat verification within a program synthesis runtime.

A rejected claim: a 15/15 adoption result was traced to the task generator and search engine having been co-designed. It was caught before publication. Read the standing gates and limitations; there is no separate postmortem for that result.

Reading the evidence

What does “validated” mean here?

On this site, “validated” means a result survives a stated counterfactual or held-out test linked from the project entry. It is not a claim of independent replication, peer review, or general applicability.

  1. Check the task and scale. Synthetic tasks and short sequences impose limits on what a result can establish.
  2. Inspect the control. Compare compute budgets and evaluation conditions before attributing a gain to the mechanism.
  3. Read the rejected results. Retractions and limitations are part of the evidence, not footnotes to skip.
  4. Follow the source. Repository documentation and run artifacts provide the detailed record behind the website summary.
